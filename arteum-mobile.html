UX/UI CASE STUDY

Arteum Mobile

Making museum visits easier through clear navigation and accessible information.

Role

UX/UI Designer

Timeline

4 Months

Tools

Figma

Focus

Accessibility

Overview

Arteum Mobile is a museum companion app designed to help visitors explore exhibitions, access essential information, and plan their visit with confidence. The project focused on making museum information easier to find and understand, with particular attention to accessibility, readability, and simple navigation.

The Problem

Museum information is often spread across different pages and can be difficult to navigate. Visitors may need to search for exhibition details, opening hours, tickets, or accessibility information before planning their visit. When this information is difficult to find or presented in a cluttered way, the experience becomes more frustrating. The challenge was to create a simpler and more accessible way for visitors to discover information and plan their visit.

Research

I conducted a screener survey followed by semi-structured interviews with people who regularly use digital products and have experience attending cultural events or visiting museums. The interviews focused on how participants plan museum visits, what information they look for, how they currently use museum websites, and what difficulties they encounter. Rather than assuming users needed more features, I wanted to understand which information and interactions were actually useful to them.

Key Insights

1 - Essential information should be easy to find Participants highlighted the importance of quickly accessing practical information such as exhibitions, tickets, opening hours, and accessibility details. 2 - Readability affects the experience Participants mentioned small text, distracting interfaces, and difficult colour combinations as barriers to comfortable use. 3 - Clear and concise information is easier to process Participants preferred compact, clearly structured information instead of long blocks of text. 4 - Digital tools can support visit planning Participants used museum websites to find information and, in some cases, book tickets in advance.

Wireframes

Based on the research insights, I focused on reducing complexity and making essential information easier to access. I created paper and digital wireframes to explore different ways of structuring the main navigation, exhibition information, and key actions such as finding exhibitions and purchasing tickets. The goal was to create a clear hierarchy that would allow users to quickly scan the most important information without unnecessary steps.

Usability Testing

I conducted two rounds of usability testing with five participants in each round. Participants were asked to explore exhibitions, find practical information such as opening hours, and attempt to purchase tickets through the app. Round 1 The first round highlighted issues with visual identity and information visibility. Participants also wanted more contextual information earlier in the experience. Round 2 After iterating on the design, the second round highlighted the need for clearer navigation, better visibility of essential information, and a cleaner layout.

Final Design

Based on the feedback, I made several changes to improve clarity and ease of use. I added short descriptions to exhibition cards so users could understand the content earlier without opening another screen. I also added an additional ticket-purchasing button after participants found it unclear where tickets could be purchased. Navigation and content placement were refined to make important information easier to discover and scan. I also adjusted the top bar after noticing that the iPhone Dynamic Island was covering part of the content.

Takeaways

This project taught me the importance of validating assumptions through research instead of relying only on my initial ideas. The interviews and usability testing showed me how much impact simple things such as clear navigation, readable content, and visible actions can have on the overall experience. It also reinforced the value of iterative design - testing an idea, identifying what does not work, and refining it based on feedback.
